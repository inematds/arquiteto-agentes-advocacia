Professional workbook

In legal practice, fear of AI has a name: hallucination. The fear is justified and points to the solution. The agent reads, counts, and prepares; the attorney decides, develops the legal argument, and signs. Citing case law from memory is neither of those: the agent marks it "to verify," and the attorney checks the source.

What you’ll know how to do

The result, not the theory

Separate preparing from signing with the first-year intern test, and list the ten law firm cases an agent can handle.

Map deadline control and standard contracts in six links, with uncertain calculations turned into a question instead of a date.

Decide on legal governance confidentiality on the first line, attorney-client privilege out of scope, one attorney responsible per row in the matrix.

Build and rehearse the specification in the kit, starting with the law firm preset, with the tough-case battery every firm has already faced.

Who it’s for

For people who manage law firms and in-house legal departments

it’s for you if

  • You’re a lawyer with your own firm or manage an in-house legal department, and want an agent to handle repetitive work.
  • You’ve already taken (or plan to take) the foundational course AI Work Architect and want to apply it to your firm.
  • You want to go to the partner with the sentence “the agent does not sign, file, send, or read sealed cases.”

this isn't for you if

  • You’re looking for an AI that drafts legal filings and cites case law: this workbook keeps the legal argument with the lawyer, by design.
  • You don’t yet know the six links, the chain, or the permissions matrix (take the foundational course first).
